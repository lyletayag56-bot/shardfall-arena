Shardfall Arena

Five against five. Three lanes, a jungle and a river between two crystals. Push your lane, take the turrets, shatter theirs.

Goal
Destroy the enemy Crystal. It is protected until all three turrets in at least one lane are down, and turrets must fall from the outside in.
Move
Left stick on touch. On a computer use WASD or right-click the ground.
Attack
Hold the red button, Space or the left mouse button, or right-click an enemy. The small buttons focus minions (C) or turrets (V).
Skills
Tap to auto-aim, or drag a skill button to aim it and release. Keyboard: hold Q E R, aim with the mouse, release. F is your battle spell. When a skill button lights up with a white rim, it has a second cast waiting: press it again before the rim runs out. A press that comes a moment early is kept and goes off the instant it can, so combos chain.
Chains
Kaela's first skill, Triple Cadence, is three presses of one button: a step and a cut, another, and then a leap that comes down on the ground and throws whoever is in front of her into the air for a moment, unable to act. Each press has 3 seconds to follow the last; the three dots on the button show how far along you are, the icon shows what the next press does, and the cooldown starts when the chain ends. Aim each cut on its own: drag the button, or just tap and she turns on the nearest enemy. Each step stops at the first enemy in her way, so she cuts from in front of them. Her ultimate, Bloodrage, puts wings of light on her back for as long as the rage lasts: when they gutter and fold, it is over.
Inspire
A battle spell for heroes who fight with basic attacks. For 5 seconds your next 8 basic attacks come 55% faster, and may go past the usual limit on attack speed; each ignores 8 Armor plus 1 for each of your levels and gives back 60 health plus 15% of your Attack and Magic. The spell button counts the attacks that are left. 75 seconds to cool.
Lanes
Before a match, choose where you play under Lane: the EXP Lane (top, on your own), the Jungle (the creep camps between the lanes), the Mid Lane, the Gold Lane (bottom) or the Roam (beside the gold laner at first, then wherever the fight is). Every hero has a lane it is made for, marked with a gold dot, but any hero may take any lane, and each hero remembers the one you gave it. Your side's bots take the four places you leave, so the team is always whole. Taking the Jungle sets Retribution as your battle spell; you may change it back. At a table of friends each player chooses a lane the same way and everyone sees the choices; if two take the same lane, the table says which place then stays empty.
Taunt
Hesta, the Brazen Herald, strikes her gong with Brazen Call, and every enemy near her is taunted: marked with a red sign over the head, they can do nothing but walk at her and hit her with basic attacks. It holds 1.5 seconds at the skill's first level and 3.5 at its last. No skills, no spell, no recall, and the stick does nothing until it ends. What shrugs off a stun shrugs off a taunt. Every hero she taunts and every blow an enemy hero lands on her adds a Ring, shown as dots on her attack button; at four her next basic attack lets the gong out around her and shields her. Her ultimate, Stand as One, puts a single shield over her and every allied hero within its ring: whatever hits any of them comes off the same shield, so it is strongest for whoever is being hit hardest. Step out of the ring and you leave it.
Roles
Miel, the Amber Hivekeeper, can be played in two roles. Choose one under her name before the match: as a Support she goes to the bottom lane beside the carry and the shop recommends a build that keeps her team alive; as a Mage she holds the middle lane alone and the build is for damage. Your team's bots fill the other places around the one you take. Each of her skills does something for allies and something to enemies at once, and her passive has two tempers that you choose between: while she tends, her heals and shields are 20% stronger (the glow about her bees turns pale and they fly unhurried); while she stings, her skills deal 20% more damage (it turns hot and they fly quick). Change it with the small round button above the ultimate, or the X key; it then keeps for a second. A Support starts tending and a Mage stinging. The badge on her portrait says which she is in: a heart or a sting. Bots play her in either role and change temper by the company they keep.
Stings
Miel's first skill, Stinger Flight, leaves a sting in every enemy it goes through, shown as a bright mote circling them. A sting lasts 8 seconds, hurts every second and slows a little. Hit the same enemy again before it runs out and there are two, then three at the most: each flight that lands adds one and starts the time of all of them again, and the damage and the slow are as many times over. Keeping the swarm on one enemy is where her damage is. A sting is not a blow: it does not interrupt a recall. Her ultimate, Queen's Flight, opens with a burst that hurts and slows every enemy in reach, puts a sting in each and heals her allies at once; then the hive stays out around her for 6 seconds.
Charge and Heave
Bram's Shield Rush no longer stops at the first hero. He runs the whole way, and every enemy in his path is hurt and pushed along in front of the shield to where the charge ends. For 4 seconds after it the skill button is lit: cast again to heave the shield up, and enemies in front of him are hurt and thrown into the air for 1 second. The second cast costs nothing. What cannot be moved (a steady hero, the Shardlord) is hurt but stays where it is.
Chat
In a match with other players the speech bubble under the minimap opens the chat: pick Team or All, then tap a ready-made line or Type… your own (on a keyboard: Enter for the team, Shift+Enter for everyone). Mute hides what the others say.
Friends
Friends opens a table that everyone who has this page open can join from their own Friends list. Each player picks a side, a hero and says they are ready; the host starts. All on one side is your team against bots; on both sides is friends against friends; bots fill the empty places. Inside Claude everyone must be signed in and have the page shared with them by name; on the open web anyone with the page can join, and a table has an invite link to send. Your own hero answers the stick at once; skills and attacks land a moment after the press, on every screen at the same time. The match cannot be paused, and if someone looks away for more than a few seconds a bot plays on for them.
1v1
1v1 is one hero a side on the middle lane: two turrets, then the crystal, no jungle. Fight a bot of the level and hero you choose, or a friend at a 1v1 table. From the sixth minute the turrets and crystals take more and more damage, so it does not drag on.
Level up
Tap the gold + over a skill, or press 1 2 3. The ultimate unlocks at levels 4, 8 and 12.
Watch
Watch, then Watch bots play, plays a full five-against-five between bots, with nobody at the controls. Each side has a skill level of its own, chosen above the button (Hard against Easy, say, or both the same), and the pause menu changes either side's level while the match is running. A director follows whatever is most worth seeing: a fight, a team gathering for the Shardlord, a base under attack. Tap a portrait at the top, or a hero on the battlefield or the plate at the bottom (it steps to the next hero), to follow someone yourself; drag the battlefield or press the minimap to look around freely; Auto hands the view back to the director. The plate shows the hero's health, mana, items, score and what that bot is doing right now, and the two lines under the minimap say what each team has called. The speed button runs the match at 1, 2 or 4 times. On a keyboard: Space pauses, A is Auto, 1 to 5 follow the blue heroes and 6 to 0 the red, the arrow keys move the view, + and − change the speed.
Replays
Every match you play is recorded. When it ends, Watch replay on the result screen plays it again, and the Watch button on the menu lists your last six matches (kept on this device only). A replay is the match itself happening again, so you can watch it from anywhere: the view starts on your own hero, a tap on a portrait or a hero follows someone else, Auto hands it to the director, and both teams are in sight the whole time, with what each of them called under the minimap. The bar at the bottom shows where you are: your kills and deaths are the tall marks, the other kills the short ones, turrets the squares and the great beasts the diamonds. Tap a mark to go to a few seconds before it, drag along the bar to go anywhere, or use the 15 second buttons (pressed several times in a row, the jumps add up); the speed button goes up to 8 times, and the button between the two pauses the match while you look around. Going back half a minute or so is immediate. A long jump, either way, has to run the match to that moment, so it takes a few seconds, with a plate showing how far it has got. On a keyboard: Space or K pauses, J and L jump back and forward, Home goes to the start. A new version of the game cannot play recordings made by an older one.
Bot teamwork
Bots play as a team, more of it the higher their skill. Their jungler ganks: it picks a lane where an enemy has come too far forward, goes round by the river to the road behind them where it can, and the laner goes in when it arrives. They answer a gank on one of their own, contest Riverback and the Shardlord when the other side starts on them, and later in the match fall back together when they are two short. From 7:30 they set up for the Shardlord: whoever is hurt goes home first, the rest gather at the pit and take it as it wakes if four of them are there (three will do with more of them standing than of you), then the whole team marches with it, hits the turret it is hitting and breaks the crystal before anything else. Against your Shardlord they gather at the turret it will reach and bring it down there. They step out of blows that mark the ground first, stay off a turret's ground on their way anywhere, never fight on a fountain, buy armour or resistance to match your team's damage, and play carefully when two or more of you are out of sight. Your own team's calls appear at the right of the screen ("Team: ganking bottom"): go along and the call counts you in. Easy bots only take the beasts and defend together; Hard bots do all of it sooner and add their roamer to ganks.
Practice
The Practice button puts your hero on the map alone with three dummies that cannot die. Open the practice tools (T) to set your level and gold, switch hero, turn cooldowns off, add dummies or a sparring bot, and bring minion waves in. Add a wounded friend puts an ally at your side who stays at half health, to try heals and shields on.
Gold
Last-hit minions and creeps, then buy from the shop anywhere (P). Complete items are forged from cheap parts: buy the parts as you can afford them and each comes off the item's price. The gold chip under your purse buys the next recommended part or item in one tap. To have another item first, press Prioritize on it in the shop, or press the item twice: the chip then works towards that item (its parts, then the item itself) before the rest of your build, and while nothing of it can be afforded it shows how much gold is still missing. Buying the item, or marking it again, ends it.
Recall
B channels for 6 seconds and returns you to the fountain to heal. Every hero waits it out in their own way.
Heal
Every hero has Heal built in, the green button above Recall (G): it restores 18% of your health and 18% of your mana over 3 seconds, once a minute, whatever battle spell you picked. At full health you can still use it for the mana.
Pressed item
The Ironwill Totem (Armory, under Defense) is an item you press. Once you carry it a button appears left of your skills (Z): for 6 seconds nothing can stun, root, slow, pull or mark you, whatever was already slowing or rooting you is cleared, and magic damage is cut by 60%. Your hero stands a third taller while it lasts, so nobody can miss it (the size is for show: it is no easier to hit). Each use is a second shorter, down to 4, and it takes 90 seconds to come back, so save it for the fight that matters. Bots carry it too.
Victor's Hourglass
An item for whoever lives by their ultimate (Armory, under Magic): Magic Power, mana and 15% cooldown reduction, and every kill or assist takes 30% off what is left of your ultimate's cooldown. A fight that goes well gives the ultimate back sooner for the next.
Death recap
When you are slain, Death recap (N) shows who hurt you in the last ten seconds, with which skills, how much of it was physical, magic or true damage, and what stunned, rooted or slowed you. Replay plays those last seconds again from where you stood, slowing down for the killing blow; the match goes on meanwhile, and Skip (Esc) takes you back at once. The last recap stays in the pause menu.
Looking around
Drag a finger across the open battlefield (anywhere right of the stick) and the view goes the way you drag: drag right to see what lies to the right. Let go and it returns to your hero. How far it travels is Drag sensitivity in the pause menu: slide it, or tap − and +. With a mouse, drag with the middle button. Holding the minimap jumps the view to that spot.
Aim camera
When you drag a skill button to aim a skill that reaches past the edge of the screen, the view leans after your aim so you can see where it will land, and comes back when you let go. It keeps your hero in view, so the very longest shots still run off the screen when aimed up or down. Switch it off in the pause menu if you prefer a still camera.
Cooldowns
Skills, your battle spell and Heal keep cooling down while you wait to respawn.
View
Scroll the mouse wheel to zoom and hold the minimap to look around. Camera distance and graphics quality are in the pause menu.
Phones
The game is always played sideways: hold your phone in landscape. If it shows upside down for the way you hold it, use Flip screen in the pause menu. Every control works under its own finger, so you can move, aim, attack, level up and shop at the same time.
Jungle
The Azure Golem grants cooldown and mana, the Crimson Brute makes attacks burn and slow. Stonehides in the dens near the middle pay well, and the Reedskitters in the river restore health and mana to whoever kills them. Slay the Shardlord and it marches a lane for your team.
Turrets
A turret guards its stretch of lane only, shown by a red outline when you are close: it never shoots into the jungle or the river. Hit it from anywhere and it answers. Bring minions in first: it shoots them before you. Its shots are heavy, and each one on the same hero lands 40% harder than the last, so do not stand under it alone.
Tall grass
Standing in tall grass hides you from enemies unless they step into the same grass, come right up to you, or you attack. While you stand in it your hero is drawn see-through, and so is anyone else in grass whom you can see; the grass itself never changes.
Gold plating
Until 5:00 the outer turret of every lane wears four gold plates over its own health, shown as gold bands on the tower and a gold bar above it. The plating takes every blow first, and each hit you land on an enemy turret's plating pays you gold on the spot: 100 gold a plate, 400 for stripping a turret bare. Minions wear it down too, and nobody is paid for that, so get your own hits in. Whatever is still on at 5:00 crumbles, and its gold with it.
Skins
Every hero has three looks. Pick one under the hero's name: it changes the model, the weapon and the colour of every skill. An Epic skin (Vex and Tavi each have one) goes further and brings its own moves and effects.
Skipping leaves
Orin throws leaves of iron, and each one skips from what it hits to up to four more enemies nearby, weaker each time. His shield makes him faster and impossible to stun while it holds, and grows as he deals damage. His ultimate only opens on a hero: it roots them, bursts, and leaps on to any other enemy hero standing close, so keep apart when you see it coming.
Two forms
Kiri wears two masks. Her ultimate, known from level 1, turns her between the Heron (ranged: wards that catch whoever walks into them, and a long arrow) and the Tiger (hand to hand: a leap and a rush). Her skill buttons change with her, and each turn makes the new form's two skills ready at once.
